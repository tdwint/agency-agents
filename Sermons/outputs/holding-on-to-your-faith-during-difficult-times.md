# Holding On to Your Faith During Difficult Times

**Speaker:** Keith Smith
**Date:** —
**Series:** —
**Primary Scripture:** Psalm 13:1, John 11, Hebrews 11:6

---

## Sermon Outline

### Introduction: Why Do We Suffer?
- Have you ever asked God, "Why are you allowing me or my loved ones to suffer like this?" If you've gone through emotional, spiritual, or physical pain, chances are you've asked that question
- The Bible gives many reasons for suffering: the nature of sin in the world, the building of our character, or God showcasing us to Satan the way he did with Job
- But this message isn't about why we suffer. It's about how we handle it
- **Three principles to hold on to when you're suffering physically or emotionally**

---

### I. God Is Near Even When He Seems Far Away

- When difficult times hit, we pray and ask God for immediate relief. But God answers in his own time — and that might mean days, weeks, months, or years of waiting
- After a while, we start wondering: What did I do to deserve this? Does God even care? We feel abandoned. Alone. Isolated from God
- Great men of God felt the same way:
  - **David**, running for his life from Saul, cried out: "How long, O Lord? Will you forget me forever? How long will you hide your face from me?" (Psalm 13:1 CSB). David was obedient to God and still felt like God wasn't there
  - **Job**, who lost his children, wealth, servants, reputation, and friends, experienced deep despair and isolation from God (Job 23:1-9)
- Feeling isolated from God during suffering is common and natural. But the Bible assures us: God is present even when he seems far away
- **The Emmaus Road** (Luke 24:13-35): Two of Jesus' disciples were walking home after the crucifixion, devastated and hopeless. It was the third day and Jesus hadn't risen as he promised. They'd given up. But as they walked, Jesus himself came up and walked alongside them — and they didn't even recognize him. During their worst moment, Jesus was right next to them the whole time
- If we are faithful, God is walking right alongside us during our suffering, even when we feel completely alone. "Cast all your anxiety on him, because he cares for you" (1 Peter 5:7 CSB). Does that sound like a God who doesn't care?

---

### II. God's Timing Is Perfect, Even When He Appears Catastrophically Late

- When we suffer, we want God to fix it now. But sometimes he can be agonizingly slow
- **The story of Lazarus** (John 11): Mary, Martha, and Lazarus were among Jesus' closest friends. When Lazarus became deathly ill, the sisters sent an urgent message: "Lord, the one you love is sick." They had every reason to expect Jesus would come immediately
- But days passed. By the time Jesus arrived, Lazarus was dead. Martha met him and said, "If you had been here, my brother would not have died." She may have been tempted to say, "Where have you been? You're too late. You could have saved him, but apparently there were more important things on your mind"
- But in verse 43, Jesus called Lazarus out of the tomb. He wasn't late at all. He arrived at the precise moment necessary to fulfill God's purposes. Because of what they saw, many people put their faith in Jesus (v. 45)
- God is never late. He is always on time — his time, not ours. Our suffering may be necessary and for our own good. "Suffering produces perseverance; perseverance, character" (Romans 5:3-4 CSB)
- **The Emperor Moth:** A man found a cocoon and watched the moth struggle to push through a tiny opening. Feeling sorry for it, he snipped the cocoon open with scissors. The moth emerged — but with a swollen body and shriveled wings. It never flew. The man didn't understand that the struggle was God's design. The fight to push through the opening forced fluid from the body into the wings. Without the struggle, there could be no flight
- Just as the moth needed the struggle to fly, we often need our struggles to become everything God intends for us to be. God doesn't remove our problems. He promises to be with us in the middle of them and to use them to make us stronger

---

### III. The Most Important Thing Is to Keep Your Faith and Trust in God

- When Job lost everything — sons, daughters, wealth, servants — and was covered in painful sores, it would have been easy to blame God and walk away
- Instead, Job "fell to the ground and worshipped God" (Job 1:20 CSB). He held on to his faith even when his life was full of pain and agony
- Faith matters to God: "Without faith it is impossible to please God, because anyone who comes to him must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who earnestly seek him" (Hebrews 11:6 CSB)
- We must never give up on God just because we're going through suffering. "Be faithful, even to the point of death, and I will give you the crown of life" (Revelation 2:10 CSB)
- **The dying minister:** A minister diagnosed with cancer and given only months to live preached his final sermon. He told his church: "Some of you have asked me if I'm mad at God for this disease. I'll tell you honestly that I have nothing but love in my heart for my Lord. He didn't do this to me. We live in a sinful world where sickness and death are the curse man has brought on himself. And I'm going to a better place where there will be no more tears, no suffering, and no heartache. So don't feel bad for me. Besides, our Lord suffered and died for our sins. Why should I not share in his suffering?"
- Then he sang, in an old, broken voice: *"Must Jesus bear the cross alone, and all the world go free? No, there's a cross for everyone, and there's a cross for me."*
- Those were his last words from the pulpit. He died a few days later
- That is the kind of faith we are called to have. A faith that loves God regardless of the trials we face

## Application Points

1. **Name what you're feeling and bring it to God honestly** — David and Job both told God exactly how they felt — abandoned, forgotten, in despair. You don't have to pretend you're fine. This week, take whatever frustration or grief you're carrying and lay it out before God in plain words, the way David did in Psalm 13
2. **Trust God's timing on one specific situation you've been trying to force** — Think of one thing you've been begging God to fix right now. Consider that his delay may have a purpose you can't see yet, the way Lazarus's death set up a miracle that brought many people to faith. Write it down and commit to waiting without bitterness
3. **Choose worship over blame when suffering hits** — Job's response to total loss was to fall down and worship. That's not natural — it's a decision. The next time pain or disappointment strikes, pause before reacting and consciously choose to trust God with it, even if your emotions haven't caught up yet

## Discussion Questions

1. David and Job were both faithful to God and still felt completely alone in their suffering. What does it mean to you that feeling isolated from God doesn't mean God has actually left?
2. Martha basically told Jesus, "You're too late." Have you ever felt that way toward God — like his timing missed the window? What happened next?
3. The emperor moth was crippled by someone trying to help it avoid the struggle. Can you think of a time when a difficult season turned out to be the thing that made you stronger or prepared you for what came next?
4. The dying minister said, "Our Lord suffered and died for our sins. Why should I not share in his suffering?" How does the reality of what Jesus went through change the way you think about your own pain?
5. Of the three principles — God is near, God's timing is perfect, keep your faith — which one is hardest for you to hold on to right now, and why?
