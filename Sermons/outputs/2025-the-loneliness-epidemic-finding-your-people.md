# The Loneliness Epidemic — Finding Your People

**Speaker:** Matt Chandler
**Date:** 2025
**Series:** —
**Primary Scripture:** Ephesians 2:19-22 CSB

---

## Sermon Outline

### Introduction: The Surgeon General's Warning You Never Saw

- In 2023, the US Surgeon General issued a warning — not about food, drink, or tobacco — about loneliness. The document was called "The Epidemic of Loneliness and Isolation." It argued that long-term loneliness is as dangerous to your health as smoking 15 cigarettes a day — nearly a pack
- A key line from the report: if we fail to advance social connection, "we will further retreat to our corners — angry, sick, and alone." Matt said you'd be hard pressed to find three words that better describe where we are right now
- The science backs it up: chronic loneliness increases risk of heart disease, depression, dementia, and early death. Being known and seen by others actually regulates the nervous system — empathetic listening and eye contact lower cortisol and increase oxytocin
- Key insight: humanity flourishes in small circles, not at scale. Whoever designed us knew we needed depth, not reach. We don't need to be known by everybody. We just need to be known well by some

### The Belonging Deficit

- A belonging deficit is when you feel surrounded by people but don't feel truly seen, secure, or essential
- When you're operating in a belonging deficit, specific things start happening:
  - **Hyper-vigilance for social threat.** You become alert to signs of rejection, judgment, and exclusion. You misread social cues and misinterpret questions
  - **Projection.** Matt told the story of a young woman who complained that people at her church "act like they know her when they don't." When he asked how someone would go about getting to know her, she couldn't answer. She was looking for evidence of rejection because she already felt it. The people were moving toward her — she was reading it as judgment
  - **Poor sleep and stress.** When you're always reading cynicism and exclusion into every interaction, you climb into bed at night wound up, cortisol spiking, convinced everyone is against you
- This is not God's design. God himself is community — Father, Son, Holy Spirit. He didn't create because he was lonely. Out of the overflow of his own communal perfection, he made us in his image, designed for others. The more distance we create, the more deficits build

### I. You Have Been Invited into a New Family

**Ephesians 2:19 CSB** — "So then you are no longer foreigners and strangers, but fellow citizens with the saints and members of God's household."

- A transfer happens in Christ. We were outsiders, strangers, enemies. Through Jesus' blood, death, and resurrection, we've been made insiders. Family. Friends
- Matt shared his own story: when he became a Christian, there was natural distance from the people he used to run with. His new community was "way nerdier." Friday nights went from partying to gathering to pray. But these were his people now — the old crew wasn't concerned with him growing in godliness
- This new family is wild. Every tribe, tongue, nation, background. PhDs and GEDs. People who lost whole years to addiction and people whose biggest sin was two wine coolers. What do they have in common? New family

**What does this look like practically?**

- **Shared life, not event-driven Christianity.** Acts 2:42-47 describes thick belonging — shared time, meals, possessions, prayer, daily life. Not showing up to church once a month. Not eating guacamole, talking about the Cowboys, and going home after a quick prayer
- **It's not "Jesus and me" — it's Christ in his body.** 1 Corinthians 12:12: the body has many parts, but they're all one body. You need other people's gifts. They need yours. An individualistic faith isn't what the Bible describes
- **Bearing with one another.** Carry one another's burdens (Galatians 6:2). Love one another (John 13). Serve one another (Galatians 5). Forgive one another (Ephesians 4). Confess sins to one another (James 5). Encourage one another (Hebrews). There's even a verse that says tolerate one another — that's how low the bar goes. And Matt admitted: certain personality types bother him. He struggles with people who have low pain tolerances. But the Word says: who cares what you think — be gracious, speak life, grant forgiveness
- None of this can happen at a distance. It requires proximity, knowledge, trust, and perseverance — especially when things get hard and messy

### II. A New Foundation — A Life Built on Solid Ground

**Ephesians 2:20-21 CSB** — "Built on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Christ Jesus himself as the cornerstone. In him the whole building, being put together, grows into a holy temple in the Lord."

- Every human being has some foundational belief they're building their life on — even a hardened atheist
- Three most common foundations in our culture:
  - **Pantheism** — vague spirituality. Everything is divine and interconnected. Self-discovery and internal harmony. ("Like the movie Avatar — your hair connects to a tree")
  - **Secular humanism** — humanity, reason, and science as the ultimate source of truth and morality
  - **Materialism/hedonism** — the pursuit of pleasure, comfort, and accumulation as the highest good
- Matt's observation: many people borrow from all three, create their own blend, and then put Jesus' name over the top of it. The way they talk about Jesus and cultural issues is completely disconnected from the apostles and prophets — it's pulled from vague intuitive spirituality
- For the Christian, the foundation is the apostles (eyewitnesses commissioned by Jesus), the prophets (God's voice throughout history), and Christ as the cornerstone. The cornerstone determines the shape, alignment, and stability of the entire structure. The apostles and prophets don't stand alongside Christ as equals — they point to him. Without the cornerstone, the house falls
- **Your three closest friends reveal what you actually value.** Proverbs 13:20 CSB: "The one who walks with the wise will become wise; a companion of fools will suffer harm." Proverbs 22:24-25: "Don't make friends with an angry person... or you will learn his ways." Swap out anger for cynicism, lust, greed — the principle holds. You are becoming like your three closest friends
- 1 Corinthians 15:33: "Bad company corrupts good morals." Your closest friends should stir up affection for God, zeal for his mission, hunger for holiness. If they don't, create some distance and find people who are serious about growing

### III. A New Purpose — The Presence of God

**Ephesians 2:22 CSB** — "In him you are also being built together for God's dwelling in the Spirit."

- This isn't about the Holy Spirit dwelling in you individually. This text says that when we lean into being a new family, built on the right foundation, we become a container for the Spirit to pour out
- When the presence of God shows up: holiness and awe grow, transformation and power become common, direction and clarity occur, comfort and joy abound
- **Kintsugi:** In Japan, when a meaningful dish breaks, they don't throw it away. They put it back together with liquefied gold. The broken piece becomes more beautiful than it was before
- This is the promise for those who've felt the chronic, painful gap of the belonging deficit. God wants to take broken shards with sharp edges, smooth them by his blood and the power of the Spirit, fit them together on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, and create something beautiful out of what was shattered
- That becomes a testimony to a world that — according to its own government — is in an epidemic of loneliness, rotting from the inside out, angry, sick, and alone

## Application Points

1. **Assess your belonging deficit honestly** — Ask yourself: Do I feel truly seen, secure, and essential in any relationship or community right now? If the answer is no, or if you notice yourself constantly reading rejection into normal interactions, name it. Awareness is the first step out of the deficit
2. **Evaluate your three closest friends** — Not to judge them, but to ask: what are they stirring up in me? More hunger for God, or more cynicism? More hope, or more complaint? If you need to create some distance, do it with kindness — not a speech. Then find people who are serious about growing and ask them to run with you
3. **Move from event-based faith to shared life** — Stop treating church as something you attend and start treating it as people you belong to. Join a small group, a recovery group, a serving team — whatever puts you eyeball to eyeball with other people on a regular basis. Proximity is where belonging happens

## Discussion Questions

1. The Surgeon General's report said we'll "retreat to our corners — angry, sick, and alone." Where do you see that playing out in your own life or in the people around you?
2. Matt described a young woman who misread people moving toward her as judgment. Have you ever caught yourself doing that — interpreting someone's interest as intrusion? What was really going on underneath?
3. Acts 2 describes shared meals, shared possessions, and daily life together. That feels extreme by modern standards. What would even a small step toward that kind of community look like for you this week?
4. "Show me your three closest friends and I'll tell you what you actually value." When you think about your three closest friends, what are they stirring up in you — and is that what you want to become?
5. The kintsugi image — broken pieces put back together with gold — suggests that the mended version is more beautiful than the original. What would it look like for you to bring your broken pieces into community instead of hiding them?
