# Imago Dei — Made in the Image of God

Two things are happening in me right now. One — I'm passionate about this subject and the part of me that's meant by God to be aggressive wants to expose and destroy evil. Two — I've been pastoring for 20 years, and I can feel my heart being tender toward those who've been affected by evil in ways that the shame and guilt have nearly crushed them. Both things are flaring. If you feel that shift during this message, that's what's happening.

Open your Bibles to Genesis 1:26-27.

> Then God said, "Let us make man in our image, according to our likeness. Let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, over the birds of the sky, over the livestock, over the whole earth, and over every creature that crawls on the earth." So God created man in his own image; he created him in the image of God; he created them male and female.

---

When Lauren and I moved here, I'd never lived in a white-collar community. Daddy was military, then a long-haul truck driver. Blue-collar all the way. One of the first things I noticed was how y'all treated your dogs. Strollers. Clothes. Shoes. God gave them fur and pads on the bottom of their feet — what are you doing?

We eventually got a dog. Australian Shepherd named Gus. He was brilliant. Smarter than some people I know. He could fetch specific kids by name. He'd lay by my feet while I read the Bible in the mornings. When his health failed and we had to put him down, I broke. I mean sobbing — Audrey had to put her arm around me.

But here's what I want to draw to the forefront. As awesome as Gus was, as many memories as I have of that dog woven into our lives — he was a dog. Not nearly as valuable as any human being anywhere on earth.

Humankind stands distinct and alone in value and worth. That's not to say animals should be treated cruelly. The opposite is true. But we are fundamentally different, and that difference matters.

## What It Means to Be Made in God's Image

In the creation account, God speaks everything into existence. Let there be. Let there be. Let there be. But when he gets to us, the language shifts. It's no longer "let there be." He says, "Let us make man in our image."

This — the imago Dei, the image of God — is what makes us more valuable than any other living creature. The most severely incapable human being, whether by special needs or accident, someone who cannot contribute in any measurable way, is still far more valuable than the finest animal ever born.

Why? Because humankind alone has a moral nature and a spiritual nature. Lions don't eat antelope and then struggle with guilt at night. Animals run on instinct, not moral law. It's human beings alone who can see, worship, savor, and understand what God is doing.

Now, that doesn't mean we abuse creation. This passage says we've been given dominion — and that word means to order creation toward flourishing, not destruction. There's a responsibility of care built in. But the value distinction is real.

---

## When Does Life Begin?

If humankind alone has this moral and spiritual nature, the next question is: when does it begin?

Genesis 5:3 shows us the image of God wasn't exclusive to Adam and Eve. It passes from parent to child. All humankind, across all time, is made in the image of God.

Psalm 58:3 — "The wicked are estranged from the womb; they go astray from birth, speaking lies." That moral component is present at birth.

Job 14:4 — "Who can bring a clean thing out of an unclean? No one." Two sinful people don't produce morally neutral children. If you've been around kids, you know this. Nothing is more selfish anywhere on earth than a newborn baby. That's silly, but it's true.

And then Psalm 51:5 — "I was guilty when I was born; I was sinful when my mother conceived me." At the moment of conception, the moral and spiritual component of the human soul is present.

Science lines up. At eight weeks, a baby has unique DNA and a unique blood type — meaning it's in the mother's body but it is not the mother's body. The baby sucks its thumb, recoils from pricking, responds to sound. All organs present. Brain functioning. Heart pumping. Unique fingerprint. Life combusts at conception.

## Two Things This Means

**First — you are fearfully and wonderfully made.** You are who God made you to be, and that's enough.

What's happened is we're constantly scrolling other people's highlight reels. I used to have abs, now I've got a keg. I wish I was a better mom. This constant comparison robs you of the reality that God uniquely wired you and placed you and is pleased with what he made.

Men — what if the things your dad said weren't true? What if the issues were his and had nothing to do with you? What if you are enough? What if you are a man?

Sisters — what if you are beautiful? What if your value has everything to do with how God designed you and stepping into that design, rather than conforming to some impossible, airbrushed, filtered standard?

You are fearfully and wonderfully made. What would it be like for you to actually believe that?

**Second — this doctrine has changed the world.** It overthrew Rome. It shut down the Colosseum. It ended slave trades. It built hospitals and orphanages and governments rooted in human dignity. It elevated women, children, and the poor. The things that seem normal to us weren't normal until this came into view.

C.S. Lewis called it "chronological snobbery" — this assumption that because we're modern, we're enlightened. We look back at the Spartans throwing babies off mountains and call them barbarians. Meanwhile, in our own time, a turtle egg carries more legal protection than a human baby in certain places.

We haven't evolved. We've just learned to rename our evil and make it prettier. Every culture does this.

If we say we believe the Bible, and we say the spiritual and moral nature is present at conception, then abortion is the taking of a life. I know that's a hard statement. Hang with me.

## Nobody Gets Canceled in God's Economy

In a culture that's hell-bent on canceling everybody, in God's economy nobody gets canceled.

Moses murdered a man, buried him in the sand, hid for 40 years. God pulled him back and used him to lead Israel across the Red Sea.

David assaulted a woman and had her husband killed. Still called a man after God's own heart.

Paul — blasphemer, persecutor, violent opponent of the church. He writes in 1 Timothy 1:15-16 that Christ saved "the foremost" of sinners so that others who would believe could see the patience and mercy of Jesus and know: if him, then me too.

God pulls from the fringes those he intends to be his brightest lights. That's just what he does.

If this sermon is more triggering than helpful and you're sitting there thinking, "What have I done?" — you need to hear that Jesus just pulled out the chair and said, "Have a seat. Welcome home."

Amber Dion grew up in church. Knew the Bible. Didn't understand grace until she needed it. In college, she got pregnant. Her boyfriend said not now. At 19, she had an abortion. She married the same man, moved to Texas, and carried waves of sorrow and shame for years — convinced God could forgive her but wondering why he'd want to.

Then she read a line in a book: "If you think what you've done is so terrible that you can't be forgiven, then you're taking away from the power of the blood of Christ." That broke it open. She found a post-abortion ministry at our church and began to heal. Today she tells her story openly. What was meant for her shame has been redeemed in Christ — not a marker of shame anymore, but a trophy of grace.

That same invitation exists for you. Not to be on a video. Just to step into the light. It will eat away at you for the rest of your life if you don't.

You're not unclean. Step into the light.
