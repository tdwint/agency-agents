# The Loneliness Epidemic — Finding Your People

In 2023, the US Surgeon General issued a warning. Not about food. Not about tobacco. About loneliness.

The document was called "The Epidemic of Loneliness and Isolation." It made the argument that long-term loneliness is as dangerous to your health as smoking 15 cigarettes a day. Nearly a pack.

Here's a line from it that got under my skin. If we fail to advance social connection, "we will further retreat to our corners — angry, sick, and alone." Hard to find three words that make better sense of where we are right now.

The science is clear. Chronic loneliness increases risk of heart disease, depression, dementia, and early death. Being seen and known by others actually lowers cortisol and increases oxytocin. We are neurologically wired to be known.

And here's the thing — humanity flourishes in small circles. Whoever designed us knew we needed depth, not scale. We don't need to be known by everybody. We just need to be known well by some.

---

There's a concept called a belonging deficit. Here's the plain version: a belonging deficit is when you feel surrounded by people but don't feel truly seen, secure, or essential.

When you're in one, things start happening. You become hyper-alert to signs of rejection. You misread social cues. You misinterpret questions.

I had a conversation with a young woman this week. She doesn't go to our church. She was venting that the people at her church act like they know her when they don't. I asked, "How would someone go about getting to know you?" She couldn't answer. The people were moving toward her. She was reading it as judgment. That's a belonging deficit. She was looking for evidence of rejection because she already felt it.

And when you're drinking in the world that way — everyone's an enemy, nobody's for you — good luck sleeping at night. Cortisol spiking. Everybody's a scoundrel.

This is not God's design for you. God himself is community — Father, Son, Holy Spirit. He didn't create because he was lonely. Out of the overflow of his own communal perfection, he made us in his image, designed for others. The more distance we create, the more deficits build, the dumber we become.

Three things from Ephesians 2 about finding your people.

---

## I. You've Been Invited into a New Family

Ephesians 2:19 — "You are no longer foreigners and strangers, but fellow citizens with the saints and members of God's household."

There's a transfer that happens in Christ. We were outsiders, strangers, enemies. Through his blood, his death, his resurrection, he made us insiders. Family. Friends.

When I became a Christian, there was some natural distance from the guys I used to run with. I'd sinned at a varsity level with those men. And the new family God was carrying me into was — I'll be honest — way nerdier. What do we do on Fridays? Gather to pray. That's what we do on the weekend. But these were my people now. The old crew wasn't concerned with me growing in godliness.

This new family is wild. Every tribe, tongue, nation. PhDs and GEDs. People who lost whole years of their lives to addiction and people whose biggest sin was two wine coolers. What do they have in common? New family.

What does that look like practically? Shared life. Not event-driven Christianity. Acts 2 describes thick belonging — shared time, meals, possessions, prayer, daily life together. Not showing up once a month.

It means understanding it's not "Jesus and me." It's Christ in his body. First Corinthians 12 — the body has many parts. You need my gifts. I need yours.

And it means bearing with one another. Carry burdens. Love one another. Serve one another. Forgive. Confess. Encourage. There's even a verse that says tolerate one another. That's how low the bar goes. None of this can happen at a distance. It requires proximity, knowledge, trust, and perseverance — especially when things are hard and messy.

---

## II. A New Foundation — Build on Solid Ground

Ephesians 2:20-21 — "Built on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Christ Jesus himself as the cornerstone. In him the whole building, being put together, grows into a holy temple in the Lord."

Every human being is building their life on something. Three foundations dominate our culture. Pantheism — vague spirituality, everything's divine, self-discovery. Secular humanism — reason and science as the ultimate source of truth. Materialism — the pursuit of pleasure and accumulation as the highest good.

My experience? Many people borrow from all three, create their own blend, and slap Jesus' name on top. The way they talk about Jesus and cultural issues is completely disconnected from the apostles and prophets. It's pulled from vague intuitive spirituality.

For the Christian, the foundation is the apostles, the prophets, and Christ as the cornerstone. The cornerstone determines the shape, alignment, and stability of the entire structure. Without it, the house falls.

Now — your three closest friends reveal what you actually value. Proverbs 13:20 — "The one who walks with the wise will become wise; a companion of fools will suffer harm." Paul says it plain: bad company corrupts good morals.

You are becoming like your three closest friends. I'm not saying dump them with a speech. But ask yourself: when I leave them, am I more built up in the Lord? More hungry for his kingdom? More zealous for his mission? Or are those things lower in me?

If you can't find people who stir up those things, be that person. Show up and say, "I'm hungry for this. Anybody want to figure this out with me?" People are desperate for that. They just don't know where to go.

---

## III. A New Purpose — The Presence of God

Ephesians 2:22 — "In him you are also being built together for God's dwelling in the Spirit."

This isn't about the Spirit dwelling in you individually. This text says when we lean into being a new family, built on the right foundation, we become a container for the Spirit to pour out. Holiness grows. Transformation becomes common. Clarity shows up. Joy abounds.

I took my son to Japan this fall. There's an art form there called kintsugi. When a meaningful dish breaks, they don't throw it away. They put it back together with liquefied gold. The broken piece becomes more beautiful than it was before it shattered.

This is the promise for those of us who've felt the chronic, painful gap of the belonging deficit. God wants to take broken shards with sharp edges, smooth them by his blood and the power of the Spirit, fit them together on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, and weld them with the gold of his presence and truth.

He wants to create something beautiful out of what was shattered. And that becomes a testimony to a world that — according to its own government — is in an epidemic of loneliness, rotting from the inside out. Angry. Sick. Alone.

Don't live in the shame of your brokenness or the pain of your isolation. Step into the community Christ is building. If it's not here, find it somewhere. But find your people.
