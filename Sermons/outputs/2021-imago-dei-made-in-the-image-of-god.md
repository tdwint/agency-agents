## Sermon Outline

### Introduction: You Are Not a Dog
- Matt tells the story of moving to a white-collar community for the first time and being baffled by how people treated their dogs — strollers, clothes, shoes. He grew up blue-collar (military dad, then long-haul truck driver) where none of that happened
- His family eventually got an Australian Shepherd named Gus. Gus was brilliant — could fetch specific kids by name, laid at Matt's feet during morning Bible reading. When Gus developed health issues, they had to put him down. Matt broke down sobbing at the vet's office
- The point: as wonderful as Gus was, he was a dog. Not nearly as valuable as any human being anywhere on earth
- No matter what we feel toward animals, humankind stands completely distinct in value and worth. Not because animals should be treated cruelly — the opposite — but because humans alone carry something no other creature has

---

### I. What It Means to Be Made in the Image of God

**The text:** In Genesis 1, God speaks everything into existence — "let there be, let there be, let there be." But when he gets to humans, the language shifts. He says, "Let **us** make man in **our** image, in **our** likeness."

- Being made in the image of God (a concept called *imago Dei* — Latin for "image of God") means we are the only creatures through whom God's plans and purposes can be known and carried out
- Humans alone have a **moral nature**. Lions don't eat antelope and then struggle with guilt at night. Animals run on instinct, not moral law
- Humans alone have a **spiritual nature**. We can see, worship, savor, and understand what God is doing. No animal cares about the book of Revelation
- This doesn't mean we abuse creation. The passage says we've been given "dominion" — which means to order creation toward flourishing, not destruction. There's a responsibility of care built into it

---

### II. When Does the Image of God Begin?

- Genesis 5:3 shows the image of God wasn't exclusive to Adam and Eve. It passes from parent to child across all of human history
- The moral and spiritual nature is present from the very start of life — and according to Scripture, that start is conception
- **Psalm 58:3 CSB** — "The wicked are estranged from the womb; they go astray from birth, speaking lies." Children come out bent away from righteousness, not toward it. That moral component is already there
- **Job 14:4 CSB** — "Who can bring a clean thing out of an unclean? There is not one." Two sinful people don't produce morally neutral children
- **Job 15:14 CSB** — "What is man, that he can be pure? Or he who is born of a woman, that he can be righteous?"
- **Psalm 51:5 CSB** — "Indeed, I was guilty when I was born; I was sinful when my mother conceived me." At the moment of conception, the moral and spiritual component of the human soul is present
- Science confirms what Scripture says: at eight weeks a baby has unique DNA, a unique blood type, functioning organs, a beating heart, a unique fingerprint. The baby is in the mother's body but is not the mother's body

---

### III. Two Things This Means for Us

**Point One: You are fearfully and wonderfully made.**
- You are who God made you to be, and that's enough
- Constant scrolling through other people's highlight reels robs you of this reality. Comparison steals what God has already given you
- What if the things your dad said about you weren't true? What if those words that shaped your whole world came from his issues, not yours?
- "What if you are enough?" — said directly to the men in the room
- "What if you are beautiful? What if your value has everything to do with how God designed you?" — said directly to the women
- Stop conforming to an impossible, airbrushed, filtered standard. Step into the design God already gave you

**Point Two: This doctrine has changed the world.**
- The early church elevated women, children, and the poor — and that's what overthrew Rome
- Christians looked at the Colosseum and said, "This is evil." That conviction shut it down
- The imago Dei ended slave trades, established hospitals and orphanages, and set up governments built on human dignity
- C.S. Lewis's phrase "chronological snobbery" — the belief that we're more enlightened than past cultures simply because we're more recent. But every culture learns to hide its evil and rename it. The forms change. The evil doesn't

---

### IV. The Invitation: Nobody Gets Canceled in God's Economy

- Matt acknowledges the weight of what he's said and pivots to grace
- **Moses** murdered a man, buried him in the sand, and hid for 40 years. God pulled him back and used him to lead Israel across the Red Sea
- **David** assaulted a woman and had her husband killed. He's still called "a man after God's own heart"
- **Paul** was a blasphemer, persecutor, and violent opponent of the church. He writes in 1 Timothy 1:15-16 that Jesus saved "the foremost" of sinners specifically so that others would see the patience and mercy of Christ and believe they could be saved too
- God pulls from the fringes those he intends to be his brightest lights. That seems to be the thing he does

**Amber's testimony:** Amber Dion grew up in church, knew the Bible, but didn't understand her own need for grace. In college she got pregnant. Her boyfriend said, "Not now." At 19, she had an abortion at 12 weeks. She married the same man, moved to Texas, and lived in waves of sorrow, depression, and shame for years — convinced God could forgive her but wondering why he'd want to. Then she read a line in a book: "If you think what you've done is so terrible that you can't be forgiven, then you're taking away from the power of the blood of Christ." That broke it open. She found a post-abortion ministry called Grace Abounds at The Village Church and began to heal. She now tells her story openly — not as a marker of shame, but as a trophy of grace.

---

### Closing: Be a Community of Life
- If you're carrying this — step into the light. It will eat away at you for the rest of your life if you don't
- There are people to talk to: connection central, recovery on Wednesday nights, women and men up front after the service
- The Village Church partners with specific ministries: **Young Lives** (caring for teen moms), **Human Coalition** (helping women know their options), **Preborn** (training women's facilities), **Embrace Grace** (coming alongside women who don't know where to turn)
- "I want The Village Church to unapologetically address these things — not fearful of repercussions, but marked by compassion, love, and generosity"
- Be committed to championing life from the womb to the tomb

## Application Points

1. **Believe what God says about you instead of what comparison tells you** — This week, when you catch yourself measuring your life against someone else's highlight reel, stop and remind yourself: you are fearfully and wonderfully made, on purpose, with a specific place on the field. You don't need to be someone else
2. **Step into the light with what you're carrying** — If shame has been eating at you — whether from an abortion, a decision you pressured someone into, or something else you've never told anyone — find one safe person and say it out loud. Recovery groups, a pastor, a trusted friend. The secret loses its power when it hits the air
3. **Actively support life in your community** — Look into one of the ministries mentioned (Young Lives, Human Coalition, Preborn, Embrace Grace) or a local equivalent. Volunteer time, give resources, or simply learn what they do. Being "pro-life" means showing up, not just holding a position

## Discussion Questions

1. Matt said we live in a culture of constant comparison — scrolling other people's highlight reels and feeling like we don't measure up. Where does that show up most in your life, and what would it look like to actually rest in how God made you?
2. The sermon draws a sharp line: the imago Dei is present at conception. How does that claim sit with you, and how does it shape the way you think about the value of human life at every stage?
3. Amber carried her secret for years before finding healing. Why do you think shame thrives in secrecy, and what makes it so hard to step into the light — even when we know grace is available?
4. Matt listed Moses, David, and Paul as people God pulled "from the fringes" to become his brightest lights. What does it mean for you personally that God's pattern is to use broken people, not perfect ones?
5. The sermon challenges the church to be a "community of life from the womb to the tomb." What's one concrete way you or your small group could move toward that this month?
