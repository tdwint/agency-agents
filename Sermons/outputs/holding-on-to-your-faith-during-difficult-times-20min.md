# Holding On to Your Faith During Difficult Times

Have you ever asked God, "Why are you allowing me or my loved ones to suffer like this?" If you've gone through any kind of emotional, spiritual, or physical pain, chances are you've asked that question.

The Bible gives many reasons for suffering. We suffer because of the nature of sin in this world. We suffer so that our character can be built up. We may suffer because God is showcasing us to Satan the way he did with Job.

But this morning isn't about why we suffer. It's about how we handle it.

Three principles. If you apply them, they will hold you up when everything else is falling apart.

---

## I. God Is Near Even When He Seems Far Away

When difficult times hit, we pray. We ask God for immediate relief. But God answers in his own time. That might mean days. Weeks. Months. Years.

After a while, we start wondering: What did I do to deserve this? Does God even care? We feel abandoned. Alone.

Great men of God felt the same way. David was running for his life from Saul. He cried out in Psalm 13: "How long, O Lord? Will you forget me forever? How long will you hide your face from me?" David was obedient to God and still felt like God wasn't there.

Job lost everything — his children, his wealth, his servants, his reputation, his friends. He experienced deep despair and felt completely isolated from God.

Feeling isolated from God during suffering is natural. But the Bible assures us that God is present even when he seems far away.

In Luke 24, two of Jesus' disciples were walking toward a village called Emmaus. They had just come from Jerusalem. They were devastated. They had witnessed the crucifixion of their Lord. It was the third day, and Jesus hadn't risen from the dead as he promised. They'd given up hope and were heading home to their old way of life.

But as they walked, something happened. The Bible says, "Jesus himself came up and walked along with them." They didn't recognize him. During their worst moment — grief, confusion, lost hope — Jesus was right next to them the whole time.

If we are faithful, God is walking right alongside us during our suffering. He has not abandoned us. We are his children. He loves us and cares for us. "Cast all your anxiety on him, because he cares for you" (1 Peter 5:7). Does that sound like a God who doesn't care about your problems?

---

## II. God's Timing Is Perfect, Even When He Appears Catastrophically Late

When we suffer, we want God to fix it now. But sometimes he is agonizingly slow. His timing is always perfect.

In John 11, Mary, Martha, and their brother Lazarus were among Jesus' closest friends. One day Lazarus became deathly ill. The sisters sent an urgent message to Jesus: "Lord, the one you love is sick." They had every reason to expect him to come immediately.

Days passed. By the time Jesus arrived, Lazarus was dead.

Martha met Jesus and said, "If you had been here, my brother would not have died." She may have been tempted to say, "Where have you been? You're too late. You could have saved him, but apparently there were more important things on your mind."

Do we sometimes get angry at God because of his timing?

But in verse 43, Jesus called Lazarus out of the tomb. He wasn't late at all. He arrived at the precise moment necessary to fulfill God's purposes. Because of what they saw that day, many people put their faith in Jesus.

God is never late. He is always on time — his time, not ours. Our suffering may be necessary and for our own good. Romans 5:3-4 tells us that suffering produces perseverance, and perseverance produces character.

A man once found a cocoon of an emperor moth and took it home to watch the moth emerge. A small opening appeared, and he watched for hours as the moth struggled to force its body through. It seemed stuck. Out of kindness, the man took scissors and snipped the cocoon open.

The moth emerged — but with a swollen body and shriveled wings. It never flew. Not for the rest of its life.

The man didn't understand that the struggle was the point. The fight to push through that tiny opening forced fluid from the body into the wings. Without the struggle, there could be no flight.

We often wish God would remove our struggles. But just as that man crippled the moth by removing the resistance, we would be crippled if God removed ours. He doesn't take away our problems. He promises to be with us in the middle of them and to use them to make us stronger.

---

## III. Keep Your Faith and Trust in God

When Job lost everything and was covered in painful sores from head to foot, it would have been easy to blame God and walk away.

Instead, Job fell to the ground and worshipped.

He held on to his faith even when his life was full of pain and agony. Faith matters to God. Hebrews 11:6 says, "Without faith it is impossible to please God, because anyone who comes to him must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who earnestly seek him."

We must never give up on God just because we're going through suffering. "Be faithful, even to the point of death, and I will give you the crown of life" (Revelation 2:10).

A minister found out he had cancer and only a few months to live. On his final Sunday in his church, he preached a sermon and talked openly about his impending death. He said:

"Some of you have asked me if I'm mad at God for this disease that has taken over my body. I'll tell you honestly that I have nothing but love in my heart for my Lord. He didn't do this to me. We live in a sinful world where sickness and death are the curse man has brought on himself. And I'm going to a better place where there will be no more tears, no suffering, and no heartache. So don't feel bad for me. Besides, our Lord suffered and died for our sins. Why should I not share in his suffering?"

Then he sang, in an old, broken voice:

*Must Jesus bear the cross alone,*
*and all the world go free?*
*No, there's a cross for everyone,*
*and there's a cross for me.*

Those were his last words from the pulpit. He died a few days later.

That is the kind of faith we are called to have. A faith that loves God regardless of what we face.

---

When we suffer, remember these three things. God is present, even when he seems far away. God's timing is perfect, even when he appears catastrophically late. And the most important thing we can do is hold on to our faith and trust in him.

If you have been suffering, I hope this has lifted your spirit today.
