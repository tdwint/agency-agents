# How To Be A Blessing

**Speaker:** Keith Smith
**Date:** —
**Series:** —
**Primary Scripture:** Genesis 27:31-34 CSB

---

## Sermon Outline

### Introduction: The Longing for a Blessing
- We all long to be blessed — to hear from the people in our lives that we matter to them
- The story of Brian: a young man who never heard "I love you" or "I'm proud of you" from his Marine officer father. After years of estrangement, Brian rushed to the hospital when his father had a heart attack — but arrived too late. His father died in a coma, and the words Brian longed to hear were never spoken
- The story of Esau: Isaac's firstborn son had his blessing stolen by his brother Jacob. When Esau discovered the loss, he cried out, "Bless me too, my father!" (Genesis 27:34). That same silent cry echoes in thousands of people today
- The good news: even if you haven't received a blessing — or haven't been giving one — it's not too late to start
- **Three elements of a blessing can change your life and the lives of others**

---

### I. Provide a Meaningful Touch
- When Isaac blessed his son, he said, "Come near now and kiss me, my son"
- Every blessing in Scripture involved hugging, kissing, or the laying on of hands
- Appropriate meaningful touch communicates warmth, personal acceptance, and affirmation
- Jesus modeled this consistently — he took children in his arms, put his hands on them, and blessed them (Mark 10:13-16 CSB). He reached out and touched a man with leprosy, healing him
- A hug, a hand on the shoulder, a handshake — these simple gestures can make a real difference in someone's life
- **"I know that, Daddy, but right now I need someone with skin on!"** — a little girl during a thunderstorm, reminding us that God's love often reaches people through human touch

---

### II. Offer Spoken Words of Encouragement
- "Sticks and stones may break my bones, but words will never hurt me" is a lie. Words carry incredible power to build up or tear down
- Supporting scripture: "With the tongue we praise our Lord and Father, and with it we curse men, who have been made in God's likeness" (James 3:9 CSB)
- In the Old Testament, blessings were always spoken aloud — Abraham to Isaac, Isaac to Jacob, Jacob to each of his twelve sons and two grandchildren. A blessing is not a blessing unless it is spoken
- Have you told your spouse how much you love and appreciate them? Have you told your kids or grandkids how proud you are of them? Have you thanked your co-workers?
- One kind word can make a huge difference
- **Personal story:** In eighth grade, Keith tried out for basketball — short, skinny, and up against doubters. A parent told his coach, "Keith Smith will never be a basketball player. He will never amount to anything." But Coach Weiner believed in him, encouraged him, and taught him the game. Keith went on to earn Most Valuable Player in his district during his senior year and received a basketball scholarship to Dallas Baptist University — all made possible by a coach's blessing

---

### III. Demonstrate Commitment
- Meaningful touch and encouraging words are important, but the mortar that holds them together is active commitment — a willingness to see the blessing come to pass
- Supporting scripture: "If a brother or sister is naked and destitute of daily food, and one of you says to them, 'Depart in peace, be warmed and filled,' but you do not give them the things which are needed for the body, what does it profit?" (James 2:15-16 CSB)
- Words are useless without action. A blessing is not complete without commitment to follow through
- Coach Weiner didn't just encourage Keith with words — he taught him how to shoot, pass, and lead. He invested time and effort to make the blessing a reality
- The Proverbs 31 woman blessed her family through industriousness, love, and daily commitment. It cost her something — she was up at dawn, working hard. The result: "Her children rise up and call her blessed; her husband also, and he praises her" (Proverbs 31:28-29 CSB)
- It takes hard work to bless others. But one day, that blessing will return

## Application Points

1. **Touch someone meaningfully this week** — Give a hug, put a hand on someone's shoulder, or offer a firm handshake. Think of one person in your life who might be starving for physical affirmation, and reach out to them
2. **Speak your blessing out loud** — Write a note, make a phone call, or say it face to face: tell someone specific what they mean to you. A blessing kept inside your head isn't a blessing yet
3. **Back your words with action** — Pick one person you've encouraged and ask yourself: what can I do this week to help that blessing become reality? Show up, invest time, follow through

## Discussion Questions

1. Brian never heard his father say "I love you" before it was too late. Is there someone in your life you've been meaning to bless but keep putting off? What's holding you back?
2. The sermon says a blessing must be spoken — it doesn't count if it stays in your head. Why do you think it's so hard for some people to say encouraging words out loud?
3. Coach Weiner didn't just tell Keith he could play basketball — he invested time teaching him how. Who in your life has backed up their encouragement with real commitment? How did that shape you?
4. Which of the three elements — meaningful touch, spoken encouragement, or active commitment — is hardest for you personally? What's one step you could take this week to grow in that area?
5. The Proverbs 31 woman's family eventually "rose up and called her blessed." Have you ever gone back and thanked someone who invested in you years ago? What would it mean to do that this week?
