# How to Worry Less and Enjoy Life More

**Speaker:** Keith Smith
**Date:** —
**Series:** —
**Primary Scripture:** Philippians 4:6-7 CSB

---

## Sermon Outline

### Introduction: The Problem with Worry
- Some people who suffer a lot worry very little, while others who suffer little worry a lot
- The Bible is clear: Jesus says "do not worry" (Matthew 6:25 CSB) — in the original language, that literally means "stop being anxious." Paul says, "Do not be anxious about anything" (Philippians 4:6 CSB)
- Three dangers of worrying too much:
  - **It damages your health.** Prolonged anxiety can lead to ulcers, headaches, backaches, changes in blood pressure, heart attacks, and depression. Worry becomes crippling if it's not controlled
  - **It disrupts your spiritual life.** When worries consume your thoughts, you leave the Lord out of your life. Less prayer. Less time in the Bible. Less interest in worship. Paul says that too much worry causes God's peace to diminish (Philippians 4:6)
  - **It affects how you treat people.** When you worry a lot, you get harsher toward your family, friends, and co-workers. You lash out at the people you love most
- "Worrying is like a rocking chair — it will give you something to do, but it won't get you anywhere"
- **Three ways to worry less and enjoy life more**

---

### I. Replace Worry with Prayer

> "Do not be anxious about anything, but in everything, by prayer and petition, with thanksgiving, present your requests to God. And the peace of God, which transcends all understanding, will guard your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus." — Philippians 4:6-7 CSB

- Turn your worries into prayer. When you do, God's peace will soothe your heart
- God wants your problems. He wants you to lay your worries in his lap. "Cast all your anxiety on him because he cares for you" (1 Peter 5:7 CSB)
- But after you give your worries to God, leave them there. Don't pick them back up and start worrying again. God will take care of it
- **Personal story:** The father of a friend was given only months to live because of heart trouble. He was on a long waiting list for a transplant. Keith asked him how he was coping. The man said he felt very peaceful, because he prayed a lot. Instead of constantly worrying about receiving a transplant, he gave his worries to God. He later received the transplant and is doing well
- Turning worries into prayer works — but you have to leave them in God's lap and refuse to let them take over your mind

---

### II. Live One Day at a Time

- Jesus says in Matthew 6:34 CSB: "Don't worry about tomorrow, because tomorrow will worry about itself. Each day has enough trouble of its own"
- We spend too much time worrying about the past and the future — playing the "what if" and "if only" games:
  - What if I get cancer?
  - What if I get fired?
  - What if my children grow up and leave the faith?
  - If only I had taken that other job
  - If only I were rich
- If you're constantly worrying about the past and future, you're wasting the present
- Planning for the future is fine. Being concerned about retirement or providing for your family — that's wise. But constantly worrying about these things is not
- Jesus tells us to live one day at a time. Each day has enough problems of its own

---

### III. Stop Worrying About Things Out of Your Control

- **What people think and say about you.** Keith shared that after preaching, he sometimes worries about what the audience thought of his message. But he can't read minds. Some will agree, some will criticize. Other people's thoughts are out of his control — so why spend time worrying about them?
- **The end of the world.** It's out of your control. "No one knows about that day or hour, not even the angels in heaven, nor the Son, but only the Father" (Matthew 24:36 CSB)
- **Death.** The fear of death is the number one phobia in the world. We are all going to die physically. Death is a part of life. For Christians, death is victory. Don't let it consume your mind
- **John Jacob Astor:** When Astor learned that his ship "The Tonquin" sank in the Pacific, he knew his investment was gone. But he still went to the theater that evening. When a friend asked how he could take such news and then attend light amusement as if nothing happened, Astor replied: "What would you have me do? Would you have me stay at home and worry and weep for what I cannot control?"
- When things happen that are out of your control, don't sit back and pout and worry. Give it to God and trust him to make things better

## Application Points

1. **Pick your biggest worry and pray about it specifically — then stop rehearsing it** — Write down the one thing that's consuming the most mental space right now. Pray about it with specifics, not vague anxiety. Then every time your mind loops back to it this week, say out loud: "I already gave that to God." The discipline is in the leaving, not the asking
2. **Catch yourself playing "what if" and "if only" and redirect to today** — For one week, pay attention to how often your mind drifts to hypothetical futures or regretted pasts. When you catch it, ask yourself: what's in front of me right now that needs my attention? Live in the day you actually have
3. **Identify one thing you're worrying about that you literally cannot control — and release it** — Other people's opinions, a medical outcome, something that already happened. Name it. Acknowledge out loud that it's beyond your reach. Then do what Astor did: go live your life

## Discussion Questions

1. The sermon says worry damages your health, your spiritual life, and your relationships. Which of those three effects have you noticed most in your own life when anxiety takes over?
2. Keith says the hard part isn't giving your worries to God — it's leaving them there. Why do you think we keep picking them back up, and what has helped you actually let go?
3. Jesus says, "Each day has enough trouble of its own." What does it look like practically to live one day at a time without being irresponsible about the future?
4. Keith admitted that he worries about what people think of his sermons. What's one area where other people's opinions have an outsized hold on you, and what would freedom from that look like?
5. John Jacob Astor went to the theater after losing his ship. Some might call that denial — others might call it wisdom. Where's the line between healthy acceptance and avoiding your feelings?
