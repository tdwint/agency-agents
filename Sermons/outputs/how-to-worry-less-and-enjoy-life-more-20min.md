# How to Worry Less and Enjoy Life More

Some people who suffer a lot worry very little. Others who suffer little worry a lot. How about you — do you have a runaway mind that gravitates toward the worries of life? Are you a worrywart?

The Bible is direct on this. Jesus says in Matthew 6:25, "Do not worry." In the original language, that literally means "stop being anxious." Paul tells us in Philippians 4:6, "Do not be anxious about anything."

Here's why this matters. Worrying too much causes real damage.

It damages your health. Medical experts tell us prolonged anxiety can produce ulcers, headaches, backaches, changes in blood pressure, heart attacks, and depression. It becomes crippling if you don't control it.

It disrupts your spiritual life. When worries consume your thoughts, you leave the Lord out of your life. Less prayer. Less time in the Bible. Less interest in worship.

It affects how you treat people. When you worry a lot, you get harsher toward your family, friends, and co-workers. You lash out at the people you love most.

Someone once said, "Worrying is like a rocking chair — it will give you something to do, but it won't get you anywhere."

Three ways to worry less and enjoy life more.

---

## I. Replace Worry with Prayer

> "Do not be anxious about anything, but in everything, by prayer and petition, with thanksgiving, present your requests to God. And the peace of God, which transcends all understanding, will guard your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus." — Philippians 4:6-7

Turn your worries into prayer. When you do, God's peace will soothe your heart.

God wants your problems. He wants you to lay your worries in his lap. First Peter 5:7 says, "Cast all your anxiety on him because he cares for you."

But here's the key — after you give your worries to God, leave them there. Don't hand them over and then pick them right back up. God will take care of it.

The father of a friend of mine was given only a few months to live because of heart trouble. He was on a long waiting list for a transplant. His chances were not good. I asked him how he was coping. He told me he felt very peaceful, because he prayed a lot. Instead of constantly worrying about receiving a transplant, he gave his worries to God. He later received the transplant and is doing well.

Turning worries into prayer works. But you have to leave them in God's lap and refuse to let them take over your mind.

---

## II. Live One Day at a Time

Jesus says in Matthew 6:34, "Do not worry about tomorrow, for tomorrow will worry about itself. Each day has enough trouble of its own."

We spend too much time worrying about the past and the future. We play the "what if" and "if only" games. What if I get cancer? What if I get fired? What if my children grow up and leave the faith? If only I had taken that other job. If only I were rich.

If you're constantly worrying about the past and the future, you're wasting the present.

Now, I do believe we should plan for the future and learn from our past. It's fine to think about retirement or plan for your family. But there's a difference between planning and worrying. Planning is productive. Worrying is a rocking chair.

Jesus tells us to live one day at a time. Each day has enough problems of its own.

---

## III. Stop Worrying About Things Out of Your Control

Three big ones.

First — what people think and say about you. I'll be honest, this is one of my biggest struggles. After I preach a sermon, I sometimes worry about what the audience thought. But I'm not a mind reader. Your thoughts are out of my control. Some people will like the message. Some won't. So why spend time worrying about it?

Second — the end of the world. It's out of your control. Jesus said, "No one knows about that day or hour, not even the angels in heaven, nor the Son, but only the Father" (Matthew 24:36).

Third — death. The fear of death is the number one phobia in the world. But we are all going to die physically. Death is a part of life. For Christians, death is victory. Don't let it consume your mind.

When John Jacob Astor heard that his ship "The Tonquin" sank in the Pacific, he knew his investment was gone. But he still went to the theater that evening. A friend who knew about the disaster asked how he could take such news and then attend light amusement as if nothing had happened. Astor replied, "What would you have me do? Would you have me stay at home and worry and weep for what I cannot control?"

When things happen that are beyond your control, don't sit back and pout. Give your worries to God and trust that he will make things better.

---

If you want to worry less and enjoy life more, do three things. Replace worry with prayer. Live one day at a time. Stop worrying about things you cannot control.

If you've been carrying something heavy, I hope this lifts your spirit today.
